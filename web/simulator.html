Definitions and model notes

Starting point. The baseline uses the paper parameterization and has no imposed vertical or horizontal limit. The values shown above define the initial counterfactual: unchanged fundamentals, commercial and residential FAR ceilings of 15, and an urban growth boundary of 20 model-distance units.

Commercial productivity
Efficiency of firms, before the model determines wages, output, and land use.
Residential amenity
Attractiveness of the city to residents.
Construction cost
Cost of producing commercial or residential floor space.
Spatial decay
Rate at which productivity or residential amenity declines with distance from the CBD.
Height/FAR limit
A physical policy input measured in effective storeys, equivalently floor space per unit of developable land.
Urban growth boundary
The maximum radius available for urban development.
Height gap
An endogenous result: the percentage shortfall in tall-building floor space under the selected height limits relative to an otherwise identical city without those limits. It corresponds to the concept estimated by model inversion in the real-world dashboard, where tall buildings are identified using the paper’s threshold of more than 14 floors. It is not the user-entered FAR limit.

Other paper parameters held fixed. Non-housing expenditure share 0.66; labour share 0.85; density-amenity elasticity −0.105; height elasticities of rents 0.07 residential and 0.03 commercial; height elasticities of construction cost 0.55 residential and 0.50 commercial; migration preference heterogeneity 6.4. The paper’s robustness exercises vary these assumptions, including expenditure and production shares, density effects, migration responsiveness, and the cost of height.
